The 6-Step ABG Interpretation Guide

Master clinical assessment with this systematic sequential framework.

Masterclass
1

Assess the pH

Check if the pH is normal (7.35–7.45). Below 7.35 indicates acidemia, while above 7.45 indicates alkalemia.

2

Evaluate PaCO₂ (Respiratory)

Examine carbon dioxide levels (35–45 mmHg). High PaCO₂ signals respiratory acidosis; low PaCO₂ signals respiratory alkalosis.

3

Evaluate HCO₃⁻ (Metabolic)

Examine bicarbonate levels (22–26 mEq/L). Low HCO₃⁻ indicates metabolic acidosis; high HCO₃⁻ indicates metabolic alkalosis.

4

Match Primary Disorder (ROME)

Use ROME: Respiratory Opposite (pH & PaCO₂ move opposite) / Metabolic Equal (pH & HCO₃⁻ move same).

5

Determine Compensation

Check if the opposing system is trying to correct the pH: Uncompensated, Partially Compensated, or Fully Compensated.

6

Assess Oxygenation (PaO₂)

Review PaO₂ (80–100 mmHg) to grade hypoxemia status (normal, mild, moderate, or severe).

Quick Memory Aid: ROME

Mastery Cheat Sheet

⚡ RESPIRATORY = OPPOSITE (pH and PaCO₂ move in opposite directions)

⚡ METABOLIC = EQUAL (pH and HCO₃⁻ move in the same direction)

Disorder pH PaCO₂ HCO₃⁻ Common Causes
Respiratory Acidosis ↓ Low ↑ High Normal COPD, Hypoventilation, CNS depression
Respiratory Alkalosis ↑ High ↓ Low Normal Hyperventilation, Anxiety, PE
Metabolic Acidosis ↓ Low Normal ↓ Low DKA, Renal failure, Severe diarrhea
Metabolic Alkalosis ↑ High Normal ↑ High Vomiting, Gastric suctioning, Diuretics
